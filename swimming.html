SWIMMING

Swimming, in recreation and sports, is the propulsion of the body through water by combined arm and leg motions and the natural flotation of the body. Swimming as an exercise is popular as an all-around body developer and is particularly useful in therapy and as exercise for physically handicapped persons. It is also taught for lifesaving purposes. Moreover, swimming is practiced as a competitive sport and is one of the top audience draws at the Olympic Games.

HISTORY

Swimming has been practiced for thousands of years, dating back to ancient Egypt around 2500 BCE. It was also practiced in Greece, Rome, Japan, and other parts of East Asia for education, military training, and competition. Although swimming became less common in Europe during the Middle Ages, it became popular again in the 17th century. By the 19th century, swimming developed into a recreational activity and organized sport, with swimming organizations and competitions established in different countries. In 1909, an international governing organization for swimming was established, now known as World Aquatics.

BENEFITS

  • Improves physical fitness: Swimming works many parts of the body, including the arms, legs, shoulders, and core.
  • Strengthens the heart and lungs: It helps improve cardiovascular endurance and breathing.
  • Builds muscle strength: The resistance of the water helps strengthen muscles without putting too much stress on the joints.
  • Helps maintain a healthy weight: Swimming burns calories and can help manage body weight.
  • Gentle on the joints: Water supports the body, making swimming a good exercise for people who want a low-impact activity.
