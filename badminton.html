BADMINTON

Badminton is a racket sport played between two players in singles or two teams of two players in doubles. The main objective is to hit a shuttlecock over a net and make it land inside the opponent's court. Players score points when the opponent fails to return the shuttlecock correctly. Badminton involves skills such as serving, smashing, clearing, dropping, driving, and net shots. Players need quick footwork and fast reactions because the shuttlecock can travel rapidly across the court. The game can be played indoors or outdoors, although competitive badminton is usually played indoors.

HISTORY

Badminton developed from older games involving rackets and shuttlecocks. Its modern form developed in British India during the 19th century and was later organized in England. The sport was named after Badminton House, where the game became popular. The Badminton Association of England was formed in 1893, helping establish standardized rules. The international governing organization was created in 1934, and badminton became an official Olympic sport in 1992.

BENEFITS

  • Improves cardiovascular fitness: Continuous movement helps improve heart health and endurance.
  • Improves agility: Quick movements around the court develop agility.
  • Improves coordination: Hitting the shuttlecock develops hand-eye coordination.
  • Improves reflexes: Fast rallies require quick reactions.
  • Strengthens muscles: Running, jumping, and hitting help develop muscle strength.
