ATHLETICS

Athletics, also known as track and field, is a collection of sports involving running, walking, jumping, and throwing. It is one of the oldest organized forms of sport and is practiced worldwide, especially in major competitions such as the Olympic Games and World Championships. Events are usually held on a 400-meter track, with jumping and throwing competitions taking place around the track. Athletics also includes related events such as cross-country running, marathons, and race walking. The rules and world records for these competitions are governed by World Athletics, making the sport organized and internationally recognized.

HISTORY

Athletics is one of the oldest sports in history, beginning with basic activities such as running, jumping, and throwing used for hunting, survival, and physical training. Organized athletics became important in Ancient Greece, where events like foot races, long jump, discus, and javelin were included in the Olympic Games around 776 BCE. Modern athletics developed further in England and became more organized in the 19th century through schools, universities, and sports clubs. The modern Olympic Games returned in 1896, making athletics one of its main sports. Today, athletics is an international sport governed by World Athletics and includes events such as running, jumping, throwing, and combined events.

BENEFITS

  • Improves physical fitness: Athletics helps make the body stronger and healthier.
  • Strengthens the heart and lungs: Running and other activities improve cardiovascular endurance.
  • Builds strength: Events such as throwing and jumping help develop muscle strength.
  • Improves speed and agility: Sprinting and other events develop quickness and body control.
  • Increases flexibility and coordination: Regular training helps athletes move their bodies more effectively.
