TENNIS

Tennis is a racket sport played in singles or doubles, where players hit a felt-covered rubber ball over a net and try to make it difficult for their opponents to return. The sport has roots in medieval France, while modern lawn tennis developed in England in the late 19th century. Its rules have remained mostly consistent, although changes such as tiebreaks and electronic line-calling have been introduced. Today, tennis is a popular worldwide sport played recreationally, professionally, and in the Olympics. Its four major tournaments are the Australian Open, French Open, Wimbledon, and US Open, each played on different court surfaces.

HISTORY

Tennis originated in 12th-century northern France from a game called jeu de paume, where players used their hands to hit the ball. Over time, it developed into real tennis and was enjoyed by European royalty, including Louis X of France and Henry VIII of England. The word “tennis” is believed to come from the French word tenez, meaning “hold” or “receive.” During the 18th and 19th centuries, real tennis became less popular as racket sports developed in England. The growth of lawn courts eventually helped shape the modern game of tennis played today.

BENEFITS

  • Improves physical fitness: Tennis involves running, jumping, and moving quickly, which helps improve strength, endurance, and overall fitness.
  • Strengthens the heart: Regular tennis can improve cardiovascular health because it keeps the heart active.
  • Improves coordination: Hitting a moving ball helps develop hand-eye coordination, balance, and reflexes.
  • Builds muscle strength: Swinging the racket and moving around the court can strengthen the legs, arms, and core.
  • Helps manage weight: Tennis is an active sport that can help burn calories and maintain a healthy weight.
